Logic model · worked example

See it filled in

Illustrative, adapted from a past EAP SDG community project. Details simplified.

1 · Resources

What we had

A team of four, three hours a week each.

A contact at the local residents' committee.

A blank wall beside a little-used reading area. Donated paint.

2 · Activities

What we did

Asked residents what they'd want on the wall (week 2).

Sketched two designs and let residents vote (week 3).

Painted together over two weekends (weeks 5–6).

3 · Outputs

What we counted

1 mural.

2 painting days.

Residents of every age who helped paint, counted at a sign-in sheet.

4 · Outcomes

What changed

More people sat in the reading area after the mural than before (counted on the same weekday, before and after).

Voice: "Older residents told us they'd visit the reading area more often now."

5 · Impact

What keeps going

The residents' committee agreed to keep the reading area open and stocked.

A short how-to so next year's fellows can run a second wall.

Check it right to left

  1. Impact → Outcomes: if this impact happens, which outcome had to happen first?
  2. Outcomes → Outputs: are these outputs enough to cause that change? What else might cause it?
  3. Outputs → Activities: does every output come from an activity with a date?
  4. Activities → Resources: do you actually have the people, time and money for each activity?

Three common gaps

  • Stopping at Outputs. "We held 3 sessions" is not a change. Your plan needs at least one Outcome.
  • An Outcome you can't see. Name how you'll notice it: a count before and after, a question you'll ask, a photo.
  • Impact with no owner. Name the person or group who keeps it going after December.

Stuck? Bring this page to your Campus Co-Directors or to Katherine Tosi (kt1992@nyu.edu). A smaller project you finish beats a big one you don't.
